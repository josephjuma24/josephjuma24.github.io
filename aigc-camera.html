01 — Problem

The Knowledge Gap Shaping AI Camera Design

As AI-powered camera and generative image technologies advance rapidly, smartphone manufacturers face a critical blind spot: insufficient understanding of how real users in emerging markets — particularly Kenya — actually perceive, evaluate, and desire these features.

Transsion Holdings, serving a large and diverse African user base through brands like Tecno and Camon, needed to go beyond usage metrics. The core challenge was understanding the why behind user engagement: Which AI+CAM concepts excite users? Which feel invasive or pointless? How much are users willing to pay, and what privacy trade-offs are they prepared to make?

Without this qualitative depth, product teams risked designing features that missed cultural context, misaligned with actual pain points, or misjudged user readiness for AI in the camera experience.

Research Objectives

Understand mobile camera usage behavior and preferences
Identify needs and pain points in photo/video editing
Explore attitudes toward AI-powered camera features
Gauge willingness to pay for AI-powered features
Assess privacy concerns around AI image data usage
Rank 23 AI+CAM concepts by attractiveness and utility

02 — Methods

A Qualitative Deep-Dive with Three User Archetypes

The study employed in-depth individual interviews (IDI) with 10 Kenyan smartphone users, purposively sampled across three distinct user segments: Content Creators (Users 1, 3, 7, 9), Photography Enthusiasts (Users 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10), and Basic Users (Users 2, 5).

Participants ranged in age from 22 to 34, held bachelor's degrees across diverse fields, and used devices spanning Tecno Camon 20 Premier 5G, Samsung Galaxy S10, iPhone 11, Vivo v29, and Oppo Reno 10. This device diversity was intentional — to capture perception differences across hardware ecosystems.

Each IDI covered photography habits, mobile phone usage patterns, photo editing behaviors, social sharing tendencies, AI familiarity, and detailed concept testing of 23 AI+CAM features. A card-sorting exercise ranked concepts by attractiveness, usefulness, and technology vs. fashion orientation.

Frequent photographers across daily life, food, nature, and events. Value AI for scriptwriting, video direction, background manipulation, and facial recognition tagging. Prefer one-time yearly AI subscriptions around Ksh 1,000 or less.

Photography Enthusiasts

Users 4, 6, 7, 8, 9 & 10

Desire emotionally engaging, high-quality images. Prefer natural lighting, varied angles, and authentic candid moments. Value AI for batch editing, image quality enhancement, and optical portraits. Prefer flexible pay-per-use or tiered monthly plans.

Basic Users

Users 2 & 5

Pragmatic smartphone users who capture daily moments and share via WhatsApp. Use AI tools like ChatGPT and Grammarly for everyday tasks. Willing to pay ~$5/month for high-value AI features; prefer annual plans for cost-effectiveness.

03 — Findings

What the Concept Testing Revealed

Most Attractive

Concept 2: Move Subjects to a New Image

Rated the most attractive concept overall. Resonated deeply across all user types — from photographers wanting perfect compositions to basic users enhancing presentations and documentation at work.

Most Attractive

Concept 20: AI Video Director

Equally top-rated. Content creators see scriptwriting and animation potential; photographers value time-saving and simplified production. Unlocks professional-grade video storytelling on a smartphone.

Most Useful

Concept 1: Recompose Photo

Voted most useful. Applications include adjusting subject distances, removing unwanted people, enhancing event photography at concerts, and creating composite scenes — valued by both pros and casual users.

Most Useful

Concept 11: Best Group Photo / Emoji Management

Equally most useful. Addresses a universal pain — blinking eyes, poor expressions, missed smiles in group shots. Praised for graduation, weddings, and baby showers where perfect group photos matter most.

Most Useful

Concept 20: AI Video Director

Tied for most useful as well as most attractive — a rare double distinction. Users across segments agreed it makes video content creation accessible, structured, and time-efficient.

Top Technology

Concept 17: AI Styling & Makeup

Ranked highest for cutting-edge technology AND stylish fashion trend — the only concept to earn both distinctions. Valued for pre-photoshoot grooming, hairstyle exploration, and fashion styling across all demographics.

01

AI Tools Are Already Embedded in Daily Life

Users actively engage with AI across 30+ applications — from ChatGPT (15% of AI+CAM usage share) and CapCut (13%) to Canva (7%), Lightroom (7%), and niche tools like ElevenLabs and Midjourney. AI is not aspirational; it is operational.

02

Video-Centric AI Is the Next Frontier

Concept 20 (AI Video Director) and Concept 21 (AI Video Image Stylization) scored high in attractiveness and technology perception. Users want AI that does more than enhance photos — they want it to produce broadcast-quality video stories.

03

AI Styling Bridges Fashion and Technology

Concept 17 achieved the rare dual distinction of "Top Technology" and "Stylish Trend." Its race-aware rendering, makeup simulation, and hairstyle visualization resonated strongly with both photography enthusiasts and basic users across gender lines.

04

Group Photo Perfection Is a Universal Pain Point

Concepts 11 (Best Group Photo) and 1 (Recompose Photo) topped the "Most Useful" rankings because they solve a problem every user experiences: imperfect group shots at meaningful events like weddings, graduations, and family gatherings.

05

Panoramic & 360° Features Lack Practical Appeal

Concept 22 (AIGC 360° Mobile Panoramic) was voted most useless despite users acknowledging its novelty. The gap between its "cool" factor and actual daily utility was too wide — users couldn't envision regular use cases beyond tourism and parties.

06

AIGC Portrait Feature: Fun with Privacy Caveats

The AIGC Portrait Feature was widely embraced as creative and fun — users envisioned it for WhatsApp profiles, social media content, and adding virtual graduation gowns to photos. However, one-third raised privacy concerns about facial image usage.

Kenyan users leverage a rich ecosystem of AI tools for photo and video creation. ChatGPT dominates as a general intelligence layer, while CapCut, Canva, and Lightroom handle visual and editing workflows. This breadth signals strong AI readiness for advanced in-camera AI features.

04 — Outcomes

User Voices & Segment-Specific Insights

The IDI sessions surfaced vivid, authentic narratives about how Kenyan users envision integrating AI+CAM features into their lives. Across all three user segments, the most valued outcomes centered on creative empowerment, time efficiency, and social expression — not raw technical performance.

Users demonstrated sophisticated expectations: they want AI to feel invisible and effortless, enhancing their natural intent rather than overriding it. Privacy concerns, while present in about 40% of users, were largely manageable when users retained clear control over data authorization.

Privacy Perception Distribution

Privacy concerns were nuanced, not binary. 60% of users expressed no worry when they retained authorization control. 20% were somewhat worried, while 20% held firm privacy concerns — particularly about facial image data and unauthorized AI learning.

The key finding: transparent, user-controlled data access dramatically reduces privacy friction. Users who understood exactly what data was being used were consistently comfortable granting access. The recommendation is clear — build explicit permission UX flows into every AI camera feature.

05 — Impact

Strategic Implications for AI+CAM Product Development

This research directly informs how Transsion Holdings — and the broader AI camera industry — should prioritize feature development for the Kenyan and African market. The findings shift the conversation from technical capability to human-centered value: users don't want AI for its own sake; they want it to solve real, recurring frustrations and unlock creative possibilities they couldn't achieve before.

The data creates a clear hierarchy: invest first in features that solve universal problems (group photo quality, subject repositioning), then build toward creative empowerment (video direction, styling), while treating privacy as a foundational design principle — not an afterthought. Subscription willingness signals a monetizable AI premium tier if value delivery is clear and transparent.

Seven Pathways Forward

01

Prioritize Group Photo Intelligence

Concepts 1 (Recompose) and 11 (Best Group Photo) topped utility rankings across all user types. Invest in best-in-class group shot composition, expression optimization, and subject repositioning as core differentiating features.

02

Build the AI Video Director

This concept achieved the rare distinction of ranking both Most Attractive and Most Useful. Its potential spans content creators to casual users — build it with scriptwriting, animation guidance, and smart transitions as a flagship AI feature.

03

Launch AI Styling with Cultural Intelligence

The race-aware rendering noted by users is a critical differentiator in the African market. Build AI Styling and Makeup with diverse, localized training data that reflects African hairstyles, skin tones, and fashion aesthetics.

04

Design Privacy-First Permission UX

60% of users are not worried about privacy — but only when they have clear control. Build explicit, granular permission flows for every AI feature that touches biometric or image data. Transparency converts skeptics into adopters.

05

Create Tiered AI Subscription Plans

70% find Ksh 1,000–2,500/month acceptable. Offer an annual plan, a group-sharing tier, and a pay-per-use option. Bundle the highest-rated features (Video Director, Recompose, AI Styling) into a clearly differentiated premium package.

06

Deprioritize 360° Panoramic in Near-Term

Concept 22 was voted Most Useless despite user curiosity. Practical utility is too low for regular use. Allocate development resources to the high-impact concepts above before revisiting immersive panoramic experiences.

07

Integrate AIGC Portrait into Social Flows

The AIGC Portrait feature has organic adoption momentum — users immediately imagined WhatsApp profile use cases and friend group sharing. Launch with native WhatsApp and Instagram sharing shortcuts, and address privacy concerns with clear one-tap consent UI.

06 — Conclusion

Kenyan users are ready for AI cameras —
if AI feels human, useful, and trusted

This research makes one thing unmistakably clear: Kenyan smartphone users are not passive recipients of AI technology — they are active, imaginative evaluators who bring sharp instincts about what is genuinely useful versus what is novelty for its own sake. The 23 concepts tested were not judged on technical sophistication alone, but on whether they solved a real problem, unlocked a new creative possibility, or enhanced a meaningful moment.

The most impactful opportunity for Transsion Holdings lies at the intersection of social photography, creative expression, and effortless AI augmentation. Features that perfect group memories, simplify video storytelling, and enable personal style exploration will resonate most deeply — especially when built with cultural sensitivity and transparent data practices. The Kenyan user is ready. The question is whether product development will meet them where they are.
